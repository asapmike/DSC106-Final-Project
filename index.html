Why climate twins?

A familiar city can make a future climate feel less abstract.

Methodology

How the Climate Twin Index is calculated

Compare seasonal profiles

For each comparison city, we compare its current climate with San Diego's projected future climate across seasons.

Standardize different units

Temperature and precipitation are transformed into component scores from 0 to 100 so that different units do not distort the final index.

Weight the components

Climate Twin Index = 0.60 * Temperature Match + 0.30 * Rainfall Match + 0.10 * Summer Heat Match.

Temperature distance = sqrt(mean over seasons of standardized seasonal temperature differences).

Rainfall distance = sqrt(mean over seasons of standardized seasonal precipitation differences).

Summer heat distance = standardized difference in summer temperature.

Project writeup

Design rationale and development process

Design decisions

We changed the project from a dense dashboard into a scrollytelling explorable explanation so the reader first understands the question, then the Climate Twin Index, then the result. The ranking uses bar length for the overall index because position and length are effective for comparing ordered values. The map is deliberately secondary: it shows where candidate cities are, but size and color encode climate similarity so geographic distance is not mistaken for climate distance.

Interaction rationale

The scenario, period, and map metric controls act as dynamic queries over the same processed CMIP6 city dataset. Clicking a city on the map or a bar in the ranking updates the fingerprint and difference charts, which supports details-on-demand. The scroll steps guide attention to one question at a time, while the controls still let viewers test whether the conclusion changes under low, moderate, or high emissions.

Development process

So far, the team has built the static webpage, loaded the processed CMIP6 dataset, implemented linked SVG visualizations, and revised the narrative around a less misleading title. The most time-consuming work was deciding how to explain “similarity” without hiding the temperature and rainfall components behind a single score. The hardest remaining design challenge is making uncertainty and model assumptions visible without overwhelming the main climate-twin story.

Data and status

CMIP6 city-level prototype dataset

The prototype uses the processed city-level JSON and CSV dataset included with the project. Records summarize selected U.S. city locations, annual San Diego temperature anomalies, seasonal temperature profiles, climate-twin similarity scores, and seasonal precipitation profiles.

The data is proposal-stage and follows the same schema as the planned Google Cloud / NOAA CMIP6 extraction output.
