# Final Project Demo Video Script

Project: Finding San Diego's Future Climate Twin
Team: Thunder4Champ - Zonglin Zhang, La Li, Vincent Gao
URL: https://asapmike.github.io/DSC106-Final-Project/
Target length: 1:55, hard maximum 2:00

## Video Strategy

Audience: San Diego residents, students, and climate-curious viewers who may know climate change is happening but do not know how to interpret future climate projections.

Goal: Show that the project turns abstract climate model numbers into a familiar comparison: which present-day city most resembles San Diego's future climate.

Main insight to emphasize: San Diego is not becoming Phoenix. Its closest present-day climate twin is Los Angeles, then Riverside. Under high emissions by the 2080s, even Los Angeles becomes a weak match, meaning San Diego starts to move beyond the climate of today's comparison cities.

Recording recommendation: Record the live GitHub Pages site at 1080p. Zoom browser to roughly 90-100% so labels are readable. Move the mouse only when clicking controls. Record narration separately if possible, then cut the screen recording to match the voiceover.

## Scene-by-Scene Script

### 0:00-0:08 - Required Opening

On screen: Hero section at the top of the page. If editing software allows it, add a small text overlay with the team members and URL.

Voiceover:
"Finding San Diego's Future Climate Twin, by Thunder4Champ: Zonglin Zhang, La Li, and Vincent Gao. The project is live at asapmike.github.io slash DSC106-Final-Project."

### 0:08-0:22 - Context and Hook

On screen: Keep the hero visible, especially the Phoenix line. Slowly move toward "Start the story."

Voiceover:
"Our audience is anyone who has heard that San Diego is getting hotter, but does not know what that future actually feels like. A common guess is Phoenix. Our goal is to test that intuition with climate data instead of vibes."

### 0:22-0:35 - Why the Visualization Exists

On screen: Scroll into the intro and Step 1.

Voiceover:
"A future temperature number is abstract. But people know what West Coast cities feel like, so comparing San Diego's future to today's cities makes climate change more concrete."

### 0:35-0:52 - Step 1: San Diego Is on the Move

On screen: Step 1, "San Diego is on the move." Show the trajectory chart. Click between 2050 and 2080s once.

Voiceover:
"Step one puts cities in climate space: warmer to the right, wetter upward. San Diego's teal path moves from today to its future, with the dashed box showing model uncertainty. But annual averages are not enough, so we need Step two."

### 0:52-1:13 - Step 2: The Closest Twin Is Los Angeles

On screen: Step 2, "The closest twin is Los Angeles." Pause on the ranking chart. Briefly drag one weight slider, then return to the default or click Reset.

Voiceover:
"Step two lets viewers choose how optimistic they are about the future, and what counts as similar. The default six-to-three-to-one weighting compares temperature, rainfall, and summer heat. The result is Los Angeles first, Riverside second, and Phoenix near the bottom. So we narrow the story to these climate triplets: San Diego, LA, and Riverside."

### 1:13-1:29 - Step 3: Watch the Climate Travel

On screen: Step 3, "Watch the climate travel." Press Play on the map. Let the marker move for a few seconds.

Voiceover:
"Step three zooms in on those three cities. Press play, and San Diego's future climate moves toward its closest match. Since LA stays ahead of Riverside, the marker mostly travels between San Diego and LA."

### 1:29-1:44 - Step 4: Three Climates, Side by Side

On screen: Step 4, "Three climates, side by side." Keep the seasonal temperature and rainfall charts still.

Voiceover:
"Step four shows why. Temperature is on top, rainfall is below, and the whiskers show model uncertainty. LA fits better because the seasonal pattern matters, not just heat."

### 1:44-1:56 - Step 5: When the Twin Runs Out

On screen: Step 5, "When does the twin run out?" Point to the final high-emissions 2080s bar with score 56.

Voiceover:
"Step five is the warning. Most best-match scores stay near eighty, but under high emissions by the 2080s, the score drops to fifty-six. Even the closest city barely fits."

### 1:56-2:00 - Closing

On screen: Scroll or cut to the takeaway headline.

Voiceover:
"San Diego is not becoming Phoenix. It is becoming more like Los Angeles, and then leaving the map."
